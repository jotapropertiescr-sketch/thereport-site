Why this exists

Every copy of the listing says the same thing.

In Costa Rica there is no title insurance to fall back on, no single MLS of record, and no national licensing body standing behind a transaction. None of that is anyone’s fault, and none of it reflects on the people working in this market. It is simply the structure.

One consequence shapes every listing you run. With no MLS, a property is commonly posted by several agents on several portals at once. The photographs are the same photographs, the description is close to the same description, and a buyer scrolling through them has nothing to tell one from another except the asking price and whoever answers the phone first. Price is the only lever most listings have left, and it is the one lever a seller least wants to pull.

What it means in practice is that when a buyer asks whether the title is clean, whether the taxes are current, whether the company holding the property is in good standing, there is no institution to point at. The answer comes down to the word of whoever is standing there. Usually that is the seller, and increasingly it is the agent, who is being asked to personally vouch for records they did not pull and have no obligation to pull.

That is a weight the market puts on you by default. A third party with no commission in the deal can take it off you.

A foreign buyer feels this more sharply than anyone. They are buying in a jurisdiction they do not know, in a language they may not read, using a registry they have never seen. The caution they bring is not personal. It is rational, and it costs you time on every single transaction, whether or not anything is actually wrong with the property.

What it is

A fixed-fee verification of the public record, commissioned before you list.

We pull the records ourselves and report what they say on the date we pulled them. Title and charges from the Registro Nacional. The registered survey plan (plano catastrado) on file, with its number, its date, and whether the area and owner on the plan agree with the folio. Corporate standing where a company holds the property. Municipal and national tax status. Every finding is dated and attributed to the source it came from. The report also carries our maintained dossier on the zone the property sits in, covering water supply, access and roads, utilities and internet actually available at the street, zoning rules, and published hazard mapping, so a buyer gets the area alongside the paperwork.

It is delivered in a form built to be handed to buyers, and the seller is free to share it with any buyer, as many times as they like, and to link it from the listing. No permission is needed each time and we are not notified when it is shared.

The questions answered before they are asked, and a listing that is not interchangeable with every other copy of it. A clean record currently earns you nothing, because there is no way for a buyer to see it without taking your word. This is how it gets recognised, in time saved and in fewer renegotiations.

On the listing itself

One line, on every posting of the property.

A report only works as a differentiator if a buyer knows it exists before they call. Put this in the listing description, on every portal where the property is posted, and it does the work whether or not anyone picks up the phone.

Copy and paste into the listing description

A buyer who sees that on one posting and not on the four others beside it has been given a reason to start with yours. They can confirm the report themselves before they contact anyone, which means the conversation starts further along than it otherwise would.

Nothing stops another agent posting the same line against the same report ID, and that is not a problem. The report belongs to whoever commissioned it, and it says the same thing wherever it is quoted. What it cannot do is say something different on one posting than on another, because the published version governs.

The fee is the same whether the buyer or the seller orders it, and the findings are the same either way. We are paid for the verification, not for the answer.

That sentence does more for independence than any amount of asserting it, which is why it is printed on the face of every report rather than buried in our terms. A seller cannot ask us to remove a finding. An agent cannot ask us to soften one. There is nothing to negotiate, and a buyer can satisfy themselves of that in about ten seconds.

Questions we expect

The fair objections, answered plainly.

I already have the documents. Why would I pay for this?

Because the documents you hold are your word, and a buyer has no way to tell your word from anyone else’s until they have paid an attorney to go and look. The same registry certificate means something different depending on who hands it over and when it was pulled. A report is a third party’s dated record of what those sources said, published at an address we control, checkable by any buyer against the ID without asking you and without telling us. That is the part your own paperwork cannot do, however complete it is.

It also saves you producing those documents again for every buyer who asks. One link, as often as you like.

Another agent already ordered one on this property. Can I order my own?

Yes, and it will be a fresh check rather than a copy. We never resell or reissue an earlier report: every order is its own engagement, pulled on its own dates, with its own ID. Two reports on the same property can differ for the ordinary reason that the record moved between the dates they were pulled, and both are accurate as of their own dates.

Anyone can look a property up by finca number on the verification page and see the Listing Edition reports published on it, newest first. The most recent reflects the latest records checked. Earlier ones show the records as of their own dates. Reports ordered by buyers never appear in that list; those stay checkable by ID only, because they belong to the buyer and not to the listing.

What if it finds something?

Then you found it on your own schedule, before an offer, when you still control the timing and the explanation. The alternative is not that nothing is found. It is that a buyer's attorney finds it in week three of diligence, when the same fact arrives as a surprise and becomes a renegotiation or a dead deal. A known issue disclosed up front is a condition of sale. The same issue discovered later is a reason to walk.

Does this slow the listing down?

Two to three business days, done remotely, before the listing goes up. That window is not conditional on anything the owner has to go and obtain, including the use-of-land certificate (uso de suelo), which municipalities take roughly ten to thirty days to issue. We deliver on schedule either way. It is the only part of the process that gets faster by happening earlier.

Do I have to do this on every listing?

No. Most agents would start with one, usually a higher-value listing or one where the ownership structure is complicated enough that questions are guaranteed. There is no retainer, no minimum and no agreement to sign.

The buyer has an attorney. Isn't this the same thing?

It overlaps and it does not replace them. A notary confirms a title is clean enough to record, which is a narrower job than checking tax standing, corporate standing, HOA finances and permits. Many attorneys do more than the minimum, and where yours does, you may not need us. Nothing in a report is a legal opinion and we say so on every page.

Are you going to compete with me?

No. We do not list property, do not represent buyers or sellers, take no commission in either direction, and accept no referral fee from anyone. A report is a fixed fee for a document. That is the entire business, and it only works if it stays that way.

Who pays, me or the seller?

Either. Most often the seller, sometimes the agent as part of how they bring a listing to market. The fee and the findings are identical regardless.

How it runs

Four steps, no meetings required.

  1. You send the property

    The finca number if you have it. A listing link or an address works too, and we confirm the match against the registry before any work starts. If the owner already holds the use-of-land certificate (uso de suelo), send it with the order and it is reported in full. If not, do not wait for one: municipalities take roughly ten to thirty days to issue them, so we deliver on schedule with that entry printed and marked not provided. Send it to us within 30 days of delivery and we reissue the report under the same ID with that entry completed, at no charge.

  2. We pull the records

    Registro Nacional for title and charges, Catastro for the survey plan, Personas Jurídicas where a company holds it, the municipality and Hacienda for tax standing. Every record pulled fresh for your engagement. Nothing reused from another file.

  3. You get the report in 2 to 3 business days

    A PDF and a published version with its own ID. Yours to share with any buyer, complete and unaltered, as often as you like. The two to three days are not conditional on any document you have to go and obtain. Anything that arrives later, the use-of-land certificate above all, is added by reissuing the report under the same ID at no charge, provided it reaches us within 30 days of delivery.

  4. Buyers confirm it themselves

    Anyone you hand it to can check it against our published version without asking you or us. That is the part that makes it worth handing over.

Limits, stated up front

What a report is not.

We would rather you know this before you order than after, and the same limits are printed in every report we issue.

  • Not a legal opinion, and not a replacement for review by a licensed Costa Rican attorney
  • Not a valuation, and not a statement that any price is reasonable
  • Not an endorsement, and never a statement that a property is a good buy
  • Not a home inspection or an engineering assessment, even where a site visit is included
  • Not a guarantee of title, and not insurance of any kind
  • Not permanent, because records change and a report describes the dates it was pulled

There are also no scores, no ratings and no risk grades anywhere in a report. The moment a document starts grading properties it becomes a judgment, and a judgment is the one thing a seller could reasonably object to. Facts, dated and sourced, are not something anyone has to argue with.

Where someone else is better placed than we are, we say so and point you to them. Coastal site work, legal opinions and structural questions all belong with people who do that for a living, and we take nothing for sending you there.
