01Scope and limitations

Edition: Area Edition  ·  Zones compared: Santa Ana and Atenas  ·  No property examined

An Area Edition is commissioned before a property is chosen. It describes the conditions of living in two zones, drawn from the public record and from direct enquiry to the bodies that supply each service, so that the choice of neighborhood is made on facts rather than on what a listing says about a view.

No property was identified and no title, corporate, tax or permit record was examined. Nothing in this report describes any specific lot, house or listing, and nothing here should be read as a finding about one.

This is not a legal opinion, not a valuation, and not a recommendation that either zone is the better choice. Conditions change, and a finding accurate on the date it was checked may not describe the position today.

About the figures in this sampleValues, dates and names here are illustrative and have been redacted or replaced. An issued report carries the actual values, the statement each one came from, and the date that statement was pulled.

03Water supply and reliability

Sources: AyA, ASADA Atenas, municipal records  ·  Checked 06 October 2026
Finding recorded

Zone A. Supplied by AyA on the national network across the districts examined. Availability letters (carta de disponibilidad de agua) are issued by AyA directly, and the status of the supply at a given address is confirmable before purchase.

Zone B. Supply is split. Part of the canton is served by AyA and part by ASADAs, which are community-run water associations operating under their own statutes. Where an ASADA supplies a lot, a new connection requires the ASADA's own availability letter, and some ASADAs operate waiting lists for new connections.

Why this mattersA lot without a water availability letter cannot obtain a construction permit. Whether the supplying body is AyA or an ASADA, and whether it is currently issuing connections, is a question to settle before committing to a lot rather than after.

Dry-season pressure reductions were reported by both bodies for parts of each zone. The affected sectors and the months stated were recorded and supplied with this report.

04Access, roads and travel

Sources: MOPT route classification, municipal road inventory, direct observation  ·  Checked 07 October 2026
Reported

Route classification for every road serving each zone, stating which are national routes maintained by MOPT and which are cantonal roads maintained by the municipality. The distinction decides who is responsible for repair and how quickly it tends to happen.

Surface condition on the access roads was observed directly and is described plainly: paved, in repair, unpaved, or seasonal. Where a route is the only access to a sector, that is stated, because a single point of access is a different proposition from two.

Travel times to Juan Santamaría airport, to the nearest hospital, and into San José were measured on two weekday mornings in each zone and are reported with the times and dates they were measured, not as averages.

05Electricity, internet and services

Sources: CNFL, ICE, Coopelesca / local distributor, operator coverage maps  ·  Checked 07 October 2026
Reported

The supplying electricity utility for each zone is named, since the distributor differs by canton and so do connection procedures and tariffs.

Fixed internet availability is reported by technology actually available at the street, fibre, cable or fixed wireless, rather than by the operator's marketing coverage map. Where a sector is served only by fixed wireless or satellite, that is stated.

Distance to the nearest public clinic (EBAIS), private hospital, pharmacy, supermarket and school is given for each zone, measured from the district centre.

06Zoning, land use and municipal rules

Sources: Plan Regulador where in force, municipal Control Constructivo  ·  Checked 08 October 2026
Finding recorded

Whether each canton has a Plan Regulador in force, what it permits in the sectors examined, and what the setback, height and coverage rules are where one applies.

Limit of this sectionA use-of-land certificate (uso de suelo) is issued by the municipality for a specific lot. This section describes the rules that govern each zone. It does not and cannot state what a particular lot is permitted to do, which is established per lot once a property is identified.

Where a canton has no Plan Regulador in force, that is stated, together with the national rules (INVU) that apply in its absence.

07Terrain, hazard and environmental constraints

Sources: CNE hazard mapping, SENARA aquifer vulnerability, SETENA requirements  ·  Checked 08 October 2026
Reported

Flood, landslide and seismic fault mapping published for each zone, with the sectors identified by the publishing body and the date of the mapping relied on.

Aquifer protection and recharge-area designations, where they apply, since these restrict what can be built and what drainage is permitted irrespective of the Plan Regulador.

Whether an environmental viability study (SETENA) is required for ordinary residential construction in each zone, and at what scale of project that requirement begins.

08Property tax and holding costs

Sources: Municipalidad de Santa Ana, Municipalidad de Atenas, Hacienda  ·  Checked 08 October 2026
Reported

Municipal property tax is levied at the national rate of 0.25% of the registered value, so the rate does not differ between the zones. What differs is the declared-value cycle each municipality runs and the additional municipal service charges applied, which are listed separately for each canton.

The solidarity tax on high-value homes (impuesto solidario) applies nationally above the threshold published by Hacienda for the year. The threshold in force on the date checked is stated, along with the obligation to declare.

Garbage, street lighting and other municipal service charges are listed per canton as published, with the date of the published schedule.

09What the public record does not tell you

Recorded as open questions, not as findings
Reported

Items that came up during the work which no public record answers are recorded here as questions rather than left out. In this sample they include the current new-connection position of a community water association, which is set by its own board and is not published, and the condition of a cantonal road in the months after the rainy season, which is observable but not recorded anywhere.

Naming these is part of the work. A report that lists only what it could confirm leaves the reader to assume the rest was checked.
